PROJECT CASE STUDY

Stock Shortage Analysis

A shortage analysis project designed to identify recurring material gaps, measure shortage severity and frequency, and support better purchase planning.

Business Question

Which materials are repeatedly short, how much stock is missing, and during which periods do shortages occur most frequently?

Objective

Create an analytical reporting system that identifies material shortages, high-risk items and recurring shortage patterns so that purchasing teams can prioritize procurement and reduce stock gaps.

Data Fields

Material, category, required quantity, available stock, shortage quantity, shortage percentage, date, month, supplier, reorder level and shortage status.

Analysis

  • Compare required quantity with available stock.
  • Calculate shortage quantity and shortage percentage.
  • Identify materials with repeated shortages.
  • Rank materials by shortage frequency and severity.
  • Analyze shortages by month and material category.
  • Identify materials requiring immediate purchasing attention.

Key KPIs

  • Total shortage quantity
  • Number of shortage incidents
  • Shortage percentage
  • Materials currently below required stock
  • Top shortage materials
  • Monthly shortage trend
  • Category-wise shortage distribution

Tools Used

Excel for data preparation, formulas, PivotTables and initial analysis, SQL for querying and aggregating shortage data, and Power BI for dashboard development and reporting.

Business Value

The analysis helps convert shortage data into actionable procurement information. It gives management a clearer view of recurring stock gaps, high-risk materials and shortage trends, helping the purchasing team prioritize materials that require attention.

Interview Story

“I built this project to move beyond simply reporting that a material was short. I wanted to identify which materials were repeatedly short, measure the severity and frequency of those shortages, and understand when and where they occurred. The final dashboard is designed to help the purchasing team prioritize procurement based on data rather than only reacting to individual shortages.”
